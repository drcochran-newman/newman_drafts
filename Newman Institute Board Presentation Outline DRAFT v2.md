# Newman Institute for AI and the Common Good
## Board Presentation Outline — Draft v2

**Audience:** Newman University Board of Trustees
**Purpose:** Secure a Board decision to launch and finance the Newman Institute for AI and the Common Good.
**Decision ask:** Approve the Institute launch and a $500,000 internal fixed-rate loan (Option 1), with quarterly accountability against six measures.
**Companion artifact:** *The Work Ahead* board web experience (v2). The deck and the experience share one narrative spine so the room hears, sees, and feels the same argument.

---

## What changed from v1 — and why

The v1 outline was sound but pitched as a mission-and-market deck that drifted toward finance. v2 sharpens it into a **decision deck with a soul**: the same financial discipline, now carried by a tighter emotional arc and one argument no competitor can answer — Newman's Catholic mission, newly affirmed by the Magisterium. Six concrete upgrades:

1. **Open on the decision, land on the conviction.** The first slide states the ask; the last returns to mission, not math.
2. **A dedicated "moment is now" beat** built on the Hanover data, framed as a closing window rather than generic urgency.
3. **The *Magnifica Humanitas* encyclical becomes a centerpiece**, not a footnote — the strategic moat made explicit.
4. **"Already earned, not speculative"** is moved early to retire launch-risk before finance is discussed.
5. **Finance is framed as discipline and protection** (Bear case binds; upside is excluded from the plan), reducing the surface area for "this is a bet on AI hype."
6. **Presenter choreography, timing variants, and an anticipated-questions appendix** are added so the delivery is as disciplined as the content.

---

## Narrative arc (one sentence per beat)

Decision → Why now → What it is → Why only Newman → Already earned → Market proof → The lane → How it works → Method → Revenue → Named accounts → Money → Scenarios → Governance → Action.

Mission and market first; operational proof and financial discipline second; the vote last. The room should feel the opportunity before it scrutinizes the model.

## Presenter choreography

- **Open cold on the ask (30 sec).** No throat-clearing. State the decision, then say you'll earn it.
- **Let the encyclical breathe.** On Slide 4, read the quotation aloud and pause. This is the emotional and intellectual hinge.
- **Hand the room a number, not a paragraph.** On data slides, speak to one figure; let the others sit.
- **Pre-empt the finance objection.** On Slide 13, say plainly: "We modeled the Bear case as the binding test, and the loan covers it."
- **Close on conviction.** End on mission language — "AI for human dignity, for responsible work, for the common good" — then ask for the vote.

## Timing variants

- **30 min (full):** all 16 core slides.
- **20 min:** combine Slides 8 and 9; move Slide 10 to appendix.
- **10 min (executive):** Slides 1, 2, 4, 5, 12/13 (merged), 16.

## Anticipated questions (Appendix F)

1. *"Isn't this chasing an AI fad?"* — It scales demonstrated work (Slide 5) into current demand (Slide 2); the Bear case does not depend on the hype cycle.
2. *"What if revenue underperforms?"* — The Bear case is the binding test; the $500K covers the ~$232K two-year deficit with contingency (Slide 13).
3. *"Why a loan, not an operational allocation?"* — Speed, scale, and accountability; it preserves upside and builds quasi-endowment (Slides 12, 15).
4. *"Why Newman over WSU or a national bootcamp?"* — Structural neutrality plus a Catholic moral framework no competitor can copy (Slides 4, 7).
5. *"What protects against key-person risk?"* — Defined ~2.1 revenue FTE and a named-account instrument, not a single rainmaker (Slide 11).
6. *"What do we get for oversight?"* — Six quarterly measures and an FY28 review gate before amortization and revenue-triggered hires (Slides 15, 16).

## Design notes for build

- 16:9 PPTX; clean executive sans (Aptos or similar) for the deck; the companion web experience uses an editorial serif (Fraunces) for display.
- Palette: deep Newman navy, white, warm gold, muted teal, restrained gray.
- Consistent top-left title system; small footer with slide number.
- One visual element per slide: chart, diagram, icon row, timeline, image, dashboard, or matrix. No bullet-only slides; no centered body text.
- Avoid tech cliché: no neon, no abstract "AI brains," no circuit boards. Use Heartland industry, classroom formation, and human work.
- Export a PDF after the PPTX for board-packet distribution.

## Quality flags

- Do not over-emphasize the $180M–$320M TAM; the proposal itself says market size is not the plan.
- Treat the appropriation and statewide grant as accelerators, not assumptions (isolated on Slide 14).
- Keep the mission language present, but land on a concrete governance-and-finance decision.
