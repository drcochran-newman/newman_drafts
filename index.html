The Newman Institute for AI and the Common Good

AI is changing the Heartland.
Newman is preparing people to meet it with wisdom.

A Catholic, regional, mission-driven institute that forms the human capacity responsible AI adoption requires — for students, workers, employers, and the communities they serve.

The moment is now

Heartland employers are already asking for this. The only question is who answers.

In March 2026, Hanover Research surveyed 151 employer training decision-makers across 13 Heartland states. The signal is unambiguous: demand is current, broad, and cross-functional, and it is forming faster than the region can responsibly absorb it.

0% rate AI training a high or critical priority
0% already deploying or piloting AI tools
0% expressed strong interest in a Newman cohort

But adoption is outrunning judgment. Organizations are buying tools faster than they are redesigning the work around them. Workers, students, and leaders are being handed new power without the formation to govern it well.

The risk was never that AI would arrive. The risk is that the Heartland adopts it without wisdom and that someone less suited to the task fills the lane Newman is built for.

The Newman answer

Not an AI lab. Not a training catalog.
An operating vehicle for responsible adoption.

The Institute helps people, organizations, and communities adopt AI in ways that dignify work, strengthen human judgment, expand opportunity, protect the vulnerable, preserve institutional wisdom, and advance the common good. The work moves through three verbs.

Convene

Bring students, faculty, staff, alumni, employers, civic leaders, and Catholic and community partners to a trusted, neutral table no single sector can build alone.

Equip

Build practical AI capability fused with verification, ethical reasoning, workflow design, and the discipline to know when not to use a tool.

Transform

Move organizations from scattered experimentation to measured work redesign, transparent governance, and durable human capacity that lasts.

Why only Newman

The hardest questions AI raises are not technical. They are human. Catholic education was built to engage them.

Three weeks before this proposal, the Magisterium named the exact work Newman is positioned to do. The encyclical treats the manner of introducing AI as a moral question — augmentation over automation, invitation over assignment, subsidiarity, and the human person as the measure of every technology. That is not a slogan the Institute adopts. It is the framework it already runs on.

A coherent moral framework

Catholic social teaching answers the questions employers actually ask: how to adopt AI without displacing the workforce that built the company, and who remains accountable when a machine is wrong.

Structural neutrality

Newman competes with neither the research universities nor the community and technical colleges. It can convene them: a trusted table, not a rival.

A proven delivery record

Oversubscribed bootcamps, applied-AI graduates already in the Heartland workforce, and federal grants deployed. The capacity exists; the Institute scales it.

Roots across rural Kansas

Established sites in Garden City, Dodge City, and Liberal place Newman where the AI transition is least served and the need is most acute.

The work already earned

This is a scaling decision, not a startup bet.

Newman has spent years building the academic, professional, and civic foundation. The Institute gives that record a permanent home, a public identity, and the structure to serve the region at scale.

Human Dignity
